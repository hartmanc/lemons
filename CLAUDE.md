# Lemons project

This repo holds race-analysis pages for a 24 Hours of Lemons team. The car is a 1993 Ford Probe.

## Lemons rules

Any advice about the car, race strategy, pit stops or spending has to fit the 24 Hours of Lemons rulebook. Check the rules before suggesting something; do not assume practices from other race series apply.

- Source: https://24hoursoflemons.com/prices-rules/
- The summary below was written from that page on 2026-10-02. It is a paraphrase, not the rule text, and the rules change between seasons.
- For anything rule-dependent, fetch the live page and cite the rule number. If the page and this summary disagree, the page wins; update this file.

### Pit stops and fueling (3.3)

- Hot pits are for fueling only. The only other work allowed there is adding ice to a driver-cooling system and cleaning the windshield. No tire or fluid checks, no adjustments, no repairs (3.3.1.2).
- So any setup change (sway bar links, pressures, alignment) happens before the race or in the paddock, never during a fuel stop.
- While fueling: kill switch off, nobody in the car or getting in or out, no other work. At least two crew fuel, in full driver safety gear with visors down, plus a crew member aiming a fire extinguisher (2.5 lb B-C or larger). Fuel goes from purpose-built jugs of 5 gallons or less over a drip pan. Nobody goes over the wall until the car has stopped.
- Fueling in the paddock during the race is not allowed unless officials say so (3.3.1.1). Track pumps, where open, are allowed (3.3.1.3).
- The rules page does not say whether mechanical work in the paddock mid-race is restricted. Ask the team or check the event page before assuming.

### The $500 budget (section 4)

- Buying and preparing the car may not cost more than $500 in total (4.1). Free or donated parts count at what the last real buyer paid (4.1.2). Paid labor counts; unpaid labor does not (4.6). Sponsorship parts and cash count (4.5).
- Exempt as safety items (4.2, 4.2.1): everything required by section 3, wheels, tires, wheel bearings, ball joints, brake components, exhaust downstream of the manifold, windshield and wipers, driver comfort and information items, fuel hoses and fittings, and fuel-system parts upstream of the pump.
- Suspension parts are not on the exempt list. Sway bars, springs, struts and end links count toward the $500. Turbos, fuel pumps, injectors and engine computers also count.
- Judges assess the car at tech. Each $10 over the limit costs one lap (4.4), with no appeal.
- Selling parts off the car offsets the purchase price, but only parts that themselves count toward the $500 (4.7). A returning car can ask for a residual value instead of redoing the whole process (4.8).

### Car eligibility and equipment

- Tires: DOT street tires with a treadwear rating of 190 or higher molded into the sidewall (2.3).
- Coolant: water only, no additives, with a catch tank (3.11.2).
- Fuel: 24 gallons maximum. An original tank must keep all its original parts unmodified (3.8.1). Gasoline, pump blends, diesel or vegetable oil only (3.8.8). A second fuel leak in a race removes the car (3.8.7).
- Exhaust: 92 dB at 50 feet at full throttle, at least one muffler or resonator, no leaks (3.9).
- Bodywork: fenders covering the tires, all doors, original hood, no structure outside the original body line (3.10.3, 3.10.6).
- Night racing needs self-lit numbers, two headlights, a tail light, a brake light and reflective tape at each corner (3.10.11.3).
- Section 3 also covers the cage, seat, harness, fire system, kill switch and driver gear in detail. Read the live text for any of those; do not rely on this summary.

### Teams and classes

- An entry needs at least two drivers, and three for races of 24 hours or longer (5.1, 5.1.4).
- Classes A, B and C are assigned by judges at tech (1.5). Complaining about the class gets the car moved up one.
- The team needs a pit board or helmet-wired radio; no loose receivers in the car (5.3).

### Driving and penalties (section 6)

- Black flags are given for contact of any kind, wheels off the pavement, pit speeding, missed flags, and lack of car control, among others (6.0). The driver is always at fault (6.1), and the whole team carries the penalty (6.2).
- Black flags escalate through the day: a talking-to, then a time-wasting penalty, then one hour, then three hours, then ejection at the fifth (6.0.1).
- A car that rolls is out of the race and its driver is out for 12 months (6.3).
- These make a car that spins or drops wheels off costly. Weigh that when judging any setup change that makes the car looser.
- Yellow means no passing and single file. Red means stop at the track edge in view of a flag station. A black flag at all stations means return to the paddock (6.7).
